--- SUMMARY ---
This study examines the driving forces of greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions in South Africa's transport sector from 2011 to 2020 using an extended STIRPAT model and nonparametric additive regression, analyzing data from nine provinces. The findings reveal that population growth and economic growth (GDP) are the primary contributors to GHG emissions, with energy intensity, passenger vehicles, and freight transport also playing significant roles, though their impacts depend on population and economic factors. The research highlights the need for regionally and temporally dynamic policies to mitigate emissions, emphasizing population management, green transportation initiatives, and investment in energy-saving technologies.
